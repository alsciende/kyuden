# HANDOFF

## Current status

A full Stronghold game is playable, from setup (mulligans included) to a victory condition, round after round. The engine is a board (see `doc/game-flow.md`): the players apply the cards with generic effect messages, which cover every card of the fixtures except those listed under "Cards not playable yet". Around it: the HTTP API, the MCP server for bots, the house bot, accounts (guests, password, Google and Discord), the queue, friendly games, spectators, the history and the replay of the games, the nobori of the players and the first trophies, which give its mons and a pattern, the tournaments (creating, registering, cancelling, adding one to a calendar, running them: locking, rounds, forfeits; their trophies), the web client (desktop and tablet, phones in landscape, installable) and the production stack, deployed at kyuden.app by each tag `vX.Y.Z` of `main`. Version 1.0 is released, after a full game played against an agent through the MCP server. How it all works is in `doc/` (index in the README); what follows is why, and what comes next.

## Decisions to keep

- **1.0 is a stable contract**: the HTTP API, the MCP tools (their names and arguments) and the event log. Adding is minor; changing or removing a message, or enforcing a rule on what is accepted today, breaks it.
- **The engine moves pieces, the players apply the cards.** Effects are accepted in any place, from any player, on any card (many cards make the opponent act during the player's action). Values that cards modify (skills, strengths, glory, dueling statistic, costs) are declared by the players, prefilled by the clients. Not checked: triggering conditions, timings of abilities, limits, restrictions, the ring effect matching the ring.
- **Randomness and visibility** are where the engine does more: every draw goes through the generator of the game, and what a player sees depends only on the state (secrets stored apart).
- **A new id only when the players lose track of a card** (a shuffle, the redistribution of the provinces); a province turned face down keeps its id. No `TurnDynastyCardFacedown`: a new id in place would hide nothing.
- **The event log is a contract**: replays rebuild games from it. A message renamed or changed stays deserializable (keep an alias) and plays the same; otherwise the games played before diverge. Games older than the salted ids (November 2026) cannot be replayed.
- **Interrupts and reactions** are accepted anywhere and ended with `EndResolution`; their windows are not modeled. The `react_to_*` places stay generous (decided before 1.0): locking them down would break the replays.
- **An effect that the framework also does is another message** (`TakeImperialFavor` vs `ClaimImperialFavor`, `PlaceFateOnRing` vs `PlaceFate`, `GrantConflictOpportunity` vs `DeclareNextConflict`): so that the scripts of the cards can name it.
- **Special actions**: the messages few cards need are `special`, offered behind a "+", those the active cards need first, the rest on demand (the safety net). The script of a card says which, never the fixtures; **a script informs, it never decides**. Approximations accepted where no message fits (Calling in Favors, Karada District, Shinjo Saddle as `move_attachment`; Frontline Engineer, Kaiu Shihobu as `put_holding_into_province`).
- **Searching a deck** is two special messages, `SearchConflictDeck` and `SearchDynastyDeck` (one per deck, like the shuffles), always the whole deck: the scripts of the cards that "search your deck" name them, so that the "+" offers the search as printed rather than a look at N cards to set; "search the top N cards" stays `LookAtCards`. The deck is shuffled afterwards by the player, with its own message (the engine does not know when the search is over). Their log tells the search, not the cards ("john searches john's conflict deck", for everybody): the cards are in params of the entry that the template does not show (`GameLog::$params`), given to the searcher only, for the window of their client. The MCP server lists up to 200 tools in a page (`pagination_limit`), every message being a tool: a client that reads the first page only must still see them all.
- **Status tokens are not honoring**: placing, discarding or moving one does not trigger what reacts to honor/dishonor. A second token of the same kind is refused, not lost. Honor tokens are counters, like fate.
- **Cards set aside**: a card removed face down goes to the pile of the sender, known to them only (Stolen Secrets must not show the stolen card to its victim); a card face down under a card is known to the owner of that card (Under Siege).
- **Given actions** (`GiveActionOpportunity`) add up for the same player, replace those given to the other, are lost when passing; the next opportunities go to the target rather than activating them at once, so that the opponent is never active in between.
- **Characters as attachments**: the place is computed from the position in the stack (the first card is the character), no flag in the state.
- **Duels**: the honor is transferred once both statistics are declared (bids changed by cards count); the statistic is prefilled without the bid, which the engine adds.
- **What is resolved**: events and abilities are recorded, not characters and attachments played (no reminder needed).
- **Mulligan** in player order (the glossary); none in the games of the house bot (the introductory game goes straight to the board).
- **Accounts**: no email nor personal data; an account is recovered through its linked providers (to be pressed on players). Sessions of accounts slide over 30 days rather than short sessions and a refresh token (stolen together, for nothing). Only bots get API keys; a human helped by an AI is a matter of the terms. The `playerId` is never a credential.
- **Independent messages** (`AppAction::$independent`) ignore the expected version: what a player does on their own where both act at once (revealing their dynasty cards, the stronghold province, honor bids, declared totals). Decided by message, not by place; passing and the effects on the board stay checked (they may answer what the opponent did).
- **Mercure announces, the clients read**: no state on the hub (secrets per player, history of the hub, cost per message).
- **Nobody plays two games at once** (replays apart); waiting in the queue, an account may watch.
- **Players in the queue** are shown (the home, the screen of the queue) even when they are at most one, matched at once: the activity of the site shows in the games in progress; the count leaves out the viewer and the candidates of an invitation, and shows "nobody" rather than nothing, so that the tile keeps its shape.
- **Spectators** are accounts only, outside the game (no event, no log, no version), and see only what is public.
- **Replay** rebuilds the game from its events on a hidden copy, for one of its players once it has ended, showing what they saw then; going back is starting again.
- **Abandonment**: the time is checked before sending the message, the handler checks the state only (so that it replays). The house bot never claims (a human pausing would lose). A game everybody left ends after an hour without a winner rather than with a loser.
- **Worker**: the tasks are on `task.bus`, without the middlewares of the actions (their transaction would hold the lock of a game); one worker (the scheduler needs a single consumer). `default.bus` keeps its name (decided before 1.0).
- **Web client**: the trash button of a card stays (decided before 1.0). - **Free play, nothing secret**: every game can be watched and is announced on Discord (the games of the house bot too: the activity of the site shows there), with no consent asked; keeping one's games private may come later, as a paid option.
- **Nobori**: the catalogue of the mons and the patterns is in enums (`Mon`, `BannerPattern`), each value with its drawing in the web client (a test checks the files); what a player has earned is in tables (`UnlockedMon`, `UnlockedPattern`), the ensō and `none` given to all and never stored. Awarded synchronously when the game is won (not a task of the worker), so that the screen of the result announces them; not in the game view (it depends on the state and the viewer only, for its ETag): the client reads `/api/self/trophies` once the game has ended. Only humans earn them; a bot of the queue fills it without earning anything. The kind of a game is stored on it: `friendly` and `training` (against the house bot), two flags rather than an enum, so that `friendly` and its uses stay as they were; the migration marks the games of the house bot played before (`HOUSE_BOT_ID`), and the events of `CreateGame` recorded before replay as standard games (only their replays, which earn nothing, see it). Each trophy is a class of `src/Trophy` (its rule) beside its case of `Enum/Trophy` (its identity, stored, its reward and its place in the catalogue), rather than one service deciding them all; what applies to every trophy (humans only, no replay) stays in `GameTrophies`, being played to the end is the choice of each rule. Adding one: `doc/services.md`, "Trophies". The end of a game no longer links to its replay ("Replay this game" was confusing there): the trophies link to it, and the history.

A tip says only what the client knows (the step, the available actions, the name of the card resolved): never what a card does, its targets or its costs, which the client does not read; the fate cost of an event is paid by the engine, everything in the text box (additional costs included) is the player's. The tips seen are in the settings of the user (`seen_tips`), so that a guest keeps them by registering and an account on every device, without a query more: read with the user by the authentication, written by the `PATCH /api/self` of the settings, once for the tips seen within 3 seconds. The catalogue is the client's (`lib/tutorial.ts`): the API checks the shape of an id only.
- **The house bot** is Kyūden no Karō, "the steward of the palace": a title rather than a name, being a bot.
- **Card database**: every pack of FFG, the Stronghold cards only: Clan War (`28-`) without its treaties (Team Conquest), Under Fu Leng's Shadow (`42-`, its number at FFG, L5C42) without the Shadowlands faction (cooperative play, no clan to build a deck). The cards of these two came from EmeraldDB (`api/cards`), its text as is (`\n` as `<br>`); a cost `X` is null; Support is kept, a keyword without effect between two players.
- **Tournaments** (step 1 of "Tournaments" below; mockup `ShNGfp7Ma6k8tazb1dBaVD`): the view of a tournament is the same for every reader, what concerns the reader is under `/api/self` (their registrations, read by every page for the alert of the cancelled tournaments). Registering and changing the deck are one `PUT` of the registration. The status stays `open` until the worker locks a tournament (step 3): until then the time decides (`Tournament::isOpenAt()`), so that nothing changes after H-1 whether the task has run or not. A deck registered in a tournament still to be played cannot be deleted (rather than marked deleted while it still has to be played); a deleted account leaves the tournaments it can still leave, those it organizes go on, their organizer anonymized. `TournamentMatch` comes with step 3, where it is used. The cancelled tournaments are listed until they would have begun. The way of the newcomers shows the band of the home too. The calendar (step 2): an event of 3 hours from the start (a game has no time limit: the end is only there for the calendars), a reminder at H-1, when everything locks; the uid is the id of the tournament, so that importing the file again updates the event; a cancelled tournament keeps its file, marked cancelled, and its page no longer offers it. A downloaded file, not a subscription: a calendar does not follow the changes on its own. Running them (step 3): the pairings are kept by bracket position (table t of a round takes the winners of tables 2t-1 and 2t), so that a match without a result leaves a bye in the next round, and no match at all when neither side has a winner; a tournament with nobody left ends without a winner. The matches of a round end at once when they can (a bye, a deleted account), so that a round may chain into the next one in the same run. The 10 minutes are checked twice: `joinMatch()` refuses once they are over, and the task decides under the lock of the queue, so that a player joining at the last second is either matched or counted present. Joining with a match replaces any other wait in the queue (an invitation of the host cancelled with it). Conceding a game to join is done by the web client only (`ConcedeGame`, then the queue), the API unchanged. The page of a tournament is read again every 15 seconds rather than through a topic of Mercure. No mockup for the queue with a match: the wait is the usual one, its texts naming the opponent and their time left. Their trophies (step 4): the rules of the trophies are of two kinds, read at the end of a game (`GameTrophyInterface`) or at the end of a tournament (`TournamentTrophyInterface`), so that every trophy keeps its class and its backfill (`app:trophies:award` reads the tournaments ended too, the end of their last match as the date), rather than an award without a rule. One trophy for finishing first in a tournament, whatever the size of its bracket (rather than one per size), earned in the game of the final, so that its end announces it, none when it was won by forfeit or by a bye. Playing in a tournament gives the tatejima pattern (Into the Lists), finishing first the dandara pattern (Emerald Champion).

## Known limitations

- **Rules not enforced**: uniqueness, Limited, "max X per conflict / round", covert, ancestral attachments; lasting effects do not end (a player takes control back with `TakeControl`).
- **Abilities of a broken province** (bug, seen in a game against an agent): `trigger_ability` of `province_card` only leaves `unbroken` (`config/packages/workflows.php`), and the province is already broken in `react_to_broken_province`: an interrupt or a reaction to its own breaking (Upholding Authority: "When this province is broken") is refused ("Cannot apply transition "trigger_ability" on workflow "province_card""), in the window as after it.
- **Glory count counted twice for the rings** (bug, seen in a game against an agent): the web client prefills the glory count with the claimed rings (`gloryCount()` of `front/src/lib/board.ts`), and the engine adds them again (`GloryCountedTransitionListener`; `DeclareGloryCount` asks for the glory "without the claimed rings"): a player of the web client with no ready character and 2 rings declared 2 and won the Imperial Favor against an agent declaring 0 with 2 rings too (4 against 2, a tie by the rules).
- **Abilities triggered from a hand** (bug, seen in a game against an agent): `trigger_ability` of `conflict_card` does not leave the hand: the reaction of Vengeful Oathkeeper ("put this character into play from your hand") is refused (`not_enabled_transition`); the agent applied the effect with `put_character_into_play`, the instructions of the server telling it to concede on a rejected action.
- **Key of an invitation after the game** (seen in a game against an agent): the key is refused (401 `invalid_token`) as soon as the game has ended, so that the agent cannot read the result (`get_game`, `list_my_games`, the last `wait_for_update`) the instructions ask it to tell. To do: keep the key valid for a while after the end (an hour: the invitation is used, the queue refuses it, the game rejects every action), then delete the keys by batch in the worker, beside `DeleteExpiredSessions` (`App\Schedule`); a batch alone changes nothing while the validity is checked against the game on each request (to check in `AccessTokenHandler`). Check `end_abandoned_game` and the reads of the other games of the bot meanwhile.
- **Stale versions in reaction windows**: passing (`PassOpportunity`) is still rejected (`stale_version`) when the opponent acted meanwhile, on purpose: the player may want to react to what the opponent just did.
- **Spectators**: same layouts as the player at the bottom, a few texts still speak to the player; players cannot refuse them.
- **Web client**: the provinces offered to a character in play are those of its controller; covert is not offered (`evaded_ids` empty); a province does not show its status token (the view has it: `personal_honor` of the province, e.g. dishonored by Return from Shadows).
- **House bot**: no conflict card, no ability, the Imperial Favor always military, never discards from its provinces, never ends a duel; its opponent waits while the worker is stopped.
- **Agents (MCP)**: the view is sent whole on each `get_game`; check that clients keep the whole `instructions` (about 9 kB).
- **Scaling**: each long-poll holds a FrankenPHP thread up to 30 s (20 threads); OAuth state, rate limiter and MCP sessions in filesystem caches (one host; MCP sessions lost on restart).
- **Security**: a Mercure token outlives its session by up to an hour; account sessions have no maximum lifetime; the user creation limit is by IP only (a tournament network may hit it); deleting an account asks no re-authentication (Symfony 8.2 brings one).
- **EmeraldDB**: Emerald Legacy cards unknown; private decks cannot be downloaded.
- **Data**: a card added to the fixtures needs its image uploaded (nothing checks it); the cards of these two packs not reviewed for the effects the players cannot apply (no script yet: special actions, timings), nor tainted cards (Corrupted, taint: not modeled); banned and restricted lists not checked; copies counted by title, not by version.
- `npm audit`: 4 high vulnerabilities in the lint tooling (`braces`), not shipped; wait for an update.

### Tournaments

Decided with the author (2026-11-18), mockups validated (`ShNGfp7Ma6k8tazb1dBaVD`: home, `/tournaments`, creation, a tournament open, without an account, locked, a round in progress, registered in a cancelled tournament). To build step by step, one branch per step; once a step is done, its architecture goes to `doc/` and its decisions to "Decisions to keep".

**The rules**

- **Creating**: any account (not a guest, not a bot) creates a tournament with its start only: no name, no description (nothing to moderate). The start is stored in UTC, chosen in the time zone of the browser by steps of 30 minutes, at least 12 hours ahead, no maximum. One upcoming tournament per organizer; no other tournament (not cancelled) starting within 12 hours of it, on the whole site, so that two tournaments never overlap (checked under an advisory lock, like the queue). The organizer is not registered by creating it: they register like anyone.
- **Registering**: an account (not a guest, not a bot) registers with one of its decks, checked no more than for a standard game; 64 players at most. Until one hour before the start (H-1), a player changes their deck or unregisters, and the organizer cancels the tournament; at H-1 everything is locked. A deck registered in a tournament still to be played cannot be deleted; once the tournament has ended or was cancelled, it is only marked deleted (`DeckDeleter`, as a deck played). An account deleted unregisters before H-1, forfeits after.
- **Locking (H-1)**: the size of the bracket is the smallest power of 2 holding the players (2, 4, 8, 16, 32 or 64), the empty places are byes (always fewer byes than players: a bye never meets a bye); fewer than 2 players cancels the tournament. The first round is drawn at random (no seeds) with the generator of the tournament (its seed stored, like a game), and kept secret.
- **Rounds**: the pairings of a round are shown when it begins (at H for the first one, once the results of the previous one are published for the others). The players still in play are invited to join the queue with their match: the queue pairs only the two players of a match (like an invitation), with the deck of their registration, mulligan on. A player in a game when the round begins is offered to concede it to join. A player who has not joined after 10 minutes forfeits; both absent, or a game ended without a winner (`EndAbandonedGame`), eliminates both, and their place is a bye in the next round. No time limit per game. Once every match of the round has a result, the next round is paired, or the tournament ends with its winner.
- **Formats**: single elimination only, behind an interface (`TournamentFormatInterface`: pair the first round, pair the next one, the winner), so that other formats (Swiss…) come without touching the rest.
- **Games**: a game of a tournament is a standard game (ranked, it counts for the other trophies: `clan_<clan>`…); watching it follows the rules of spectators, unchanged.
- **Trophies**: `tournament_game` (the tatejima pattern), a game of a tournament finished, won or lost: by honor, breaking the stronghold province, a concession (of either player) or the abandonment of the opponent; not when the player left without conceding, not a game without a winner, not a forfeit (no game). `tournament_win` (the dandara pattern), finishing first in a tournament, whatever the size of its bracket (changed with the author, 2026-11-18: one trophy rather than one per size); a final won by forfeit has no game, the trophy earned without one.
- **Pages**: public, guests and visitors included (the way of the newcomers too). The home shows a band with the counts of the tournaments in progress and upcoming (and cancelled, muted, when there is one), linking to `/tournaments`; no band when no tournament is in progress or upcoming. `/tournaments`: in progress, upcoming, cancelled. The page of a tournament (`/tournaments/:id`): its start (in the time zone of the viewer, named), its organizer, the schedule (registrations, locking, round 1), the players registered (no decks, no clans), then the size of the bracket and the rounds (results and how each match ended, bye, forfeit; the games in progress to watch; the rounds to come). Registered: the deck (to change until H-1), a link to add it to one's calendar (iCal), unregistering; the organizer cancels. Without an account: create one or sign in to register (`?next=`). A player registered in a cancelled tournament sees an alert under the header on every page but those of a game, until they unregister (allowed in a cancelled tournament).
- **Not now**: notifications (none exists out of the site), Discord announcements, MCP tools and bots.

**The steps**

1. **Done** (branch `tournaments-registration`). **Model, creation, registration**: the entities `Tournament` (organizer, `startsAt` UTC, format, status: open, locked, in progress, ended, cancelled; seed, current round, winner), `TournamentEntry` (tournament, user, deck, status in play / eliminated; unique per tournament and user) and `TournamentMatch` (tournament, round, table, two entries, the second null for a bye, the game, the winner, how it ended: game, forfeit, bye); the HTTP API to create, list, read, register, change the deck, unregister, cancel (each rule of the dates checked against the clock); `DeckDeleter` and the deletion of an account; the web client: the band of the home, `/tournaments`, the creation, the page of a tournament before it locks, the alert of a cancelled tournament.
2. **Done** (branch `tournaments-ical`). **iCal**: `eluceo/ical` (v2), a public URL of the tournament (`.ics`: calendars send no token), the link on its page.
3. **Done** (branch `tournaments-running`). **Running it**: the entity `TournamentMatch` (moved from step 1, where nothing used it) and the round and the winner of `Tournament`; a task of the worker on the schedule (`App\Schedule`, every minute) locks the tournaments at H-1 (size, byes, round 1) and starts them at H; `Candidate` carries the match, `FifoMatchMakingService` pairs its two players only, `AutomaticGameLauncher` links the game to it; a listener of the end of a game records the result of the match and pairs the next round or ends the tournament; the forfeits after 10 minutes (the same task), and of an account deleted once the tournament is locked; conceding a game in progress to join; the web client: the locked page, the rounds, "Join the queue" with the time left, the queue with a match.
4. **Done** (branch `tournaments-trophies`). **Trophies**: `tournament_game` (the tatejima pattern) and `tournament_win` (the dandara pattern), `TROPHIES.md`, the web client (`TrophyId`, `trophies.ts`), `app:trophies:award` for the games and the tournaments played before.
