# HANDOFF

## Current status

Every phase of the round is modeled: a game loops from round to round until a victory condition is met. The engine is a board (see README): the players apply the cards with generic effect messages, which now cover the cards of the Core Set but those listed under "Next steps". Around it: the HTTP API, the MCP server for bots, the house bot, accounts (guests, password, linked providers), the queue, friendly games (own bot or a friend), spectators (an account watches any game in progress), the web client and the production stack (FrankenPHP, CI, releases on `ghcr.io`), deployed at kyuden.app (DigitalOcean) by the release of each tag `vX.Y.Z` of `main`.

## Principles to keep

- **The engine moves pieces, the players apply the cards.** Effect messages (bow, honor, fate, discard, move…) are accepted in any place, from any player, on any card: about 30 cards make the opponent act during the player's action, and most effects target the opponent's cards. Values that cards modify (skills, province strength, glory, dueling statistic, costs) are declared by the players, prefilled by the clients; the engine applies the rules to them. It does not check triggering conditions, timings of the abilities of cards (cards gain and lose abilities), limits per round, attachment restrictions, nor that a ring effect matches the ring.
- **Randomness and visibility** are the only places where the engine does more than move pieces: every draw goes through the generator of the game (replay), and what a player sees depends only on the state, secrets stored apart (`pendingHonorBid` / `honorBid`).
- **A card gets a new id when the players lose track of it** (a shuffle), never otherwise: a card returned to hand or put on a deck without a shuffle stays known, as at a real table.
- **No backward compatibility of the event log**: messages were renamed several times (`EndResolution`, `PlayActionEvent`…), and older events cannot be replayed. Acceptable while no game is live; to reconsider once one is.
- **Interrupts and reactions** are accepted from any player in any place and ended with `EndResolution`; the interrupt and reaction windows of the rules (first player first, consecutive passes) are not modeled. The `react_to_*` places of the conflict are generous on purpose: to lock down once the game has been tested.
- **Accounts**: no email nor personal data; an account is recovered through the providers it is linked to, which the site should press players to link. Only a bot gets API keys, so that a program is always known as a bot; a human helped by an AI is a matter of the terms of use. The `playerId` is public, never a credential.
- **Mercure announces, the clients read**: the hub carries the version of the game, not its state, which would have to be published per player (secrets), kept in the history of the hub, and computed for every message; the delays of reading the game again are not felt.
- **Mockups first** for the screens of the web client: the board and the cards looked at (Claude artifact `GiPbGSPNcvLD2r9eFKKNp5`), the accounts, the queue, the friendly games and the spectators (`LBUBwNBPdSopj5NMj5GLTL`, row "Watch a game in progress"), the cards set aside (`LdtL4NT1SnDLTzRZpz376x`: the cards removed from the game in the bar, the cards under a card, the card opened).
- **Spectators**: an account (not a guest nor a bot: the list of the spectators names accounts) watches any game in progress, its own included, and sees only what is public (`Visibility` with no viewer). Being a spectator is not part of the game: no version, no log, no event; it is kept apart (`Spectator`, `SpectatorRegistry`), a spectator who stops reading for 45 seconds forgotten, and each arrival or departure announced (`spectators_updated`). The client shows a spectator the board of a player (the first in player order, then the other one), with their names instead of "You" and "Opp.". The end of a game is told to a spectator by the log only.
- **Special actions and card scripts**: the effect messages few cards need are marked `special` (`#[AppAction(special: true)]`: 37 of them, those that react to or undo an effect of the opponent stay basic, `TakeControl` and `TransferHonor` included) and offered by the web client behind a "+" (the card dialog, the popovers of the bars, the window of a ring), those the active cards of the player need first, every other one on demand ("Show every action", the safety net for what the engine does not know). Which card needs what is said by the script of the card (`src/CardScript`, a class per card overriding `AbstractCardScript`), not by the fixtures, so that a card can later say more than a list (conditions, skill bonuses, triggering conditions). A script informs, it never decides. The first 241 scripts were generated from a review of the texts (special actions only): approximations accepted where no message fits exactly (Calling in Favors, Karada District and Shinjo Saddle as `move_attachment`; Frontline Engineer and Kaiu Shihobu as `put_holding_into_province`; Roving Michibiku as `claim_ring`; fate from the general pool onto a ring as `move_fate`), and the effects without a message (see "Cards not playable yet") left out. The timings of the triggered abilities and whether they are granted to the card attached to moved from the card fixtures (and `LogicalCard`) to the scripts too, generated by the maker from the former fixtures (`--timing`, `--granted-to-attached`): 899 scripts, a card with any of the three; 904 since the cards into provinces (below), 905 since the conflict opportunities.
- **Status tokens**: placing, discarding or moving a token is not honoring nor dishonoring (glossary, "Personal Honor, Personal Dishonor"): `PlaceStatusToken`, `DiscardStatusToken` and `MoveStatusToken` (special) write their own entries, not `PersonalHonorChangedEvent`. `PlaceStatusToken` (`token`: `honored` or `dishonored`, the enum `StatusToken`) covers a character receiving a token without being honored (Final Whisper) and the dishonored status token of a province (Overrun, Return from Shadows, Meticulous Scout: its printed text treated as blank by the players). A province holds a status token like a character (`CardState::personalHonor`, shown on the card): placed and discarded (Untainted, Perfect Land Ethos), not moved (no card does). The engine does not check which kind goes on a province: an honored token is accepted there too, the players' business. The same rules everywhere: a token placed or moved onto a card with the other token discards both; onto a card with a token of its kind, it is refused ("cannot become honored"), rather than lost. The scripts of the 33 cards that place, discard or move one say so, the Disguised characters included (they inherit the token of the character they replace: `MoveStatusToken`, after playing them). Status tokens and honor tokens are different things (see below); the tainted status tokens are not modeled (no card of the fixtures uses them).
- **Honor tokens** on provinces and holdings are counters, like fate, not status tokens: `PlaceHonorTokens` and `RemoveHonorTokens` (special, `card_id`, `amount`), on a province (any, face down included) or a holding face up in a province, of any player; the honor of the players never changes (Distinguished Dojo gains honor by sacrificing itself: `GainHonor`, then `SacrificeCard`). A holding is in play while face up in a province: leaving it, it returns its tokens to the pool (logged), switched to another province it keeps them; a province keeps them, broken included (Public Forum not being broken instead remains a gap: "Conflicts" below). No move (no card does). The card shows their number (a vermilion badge); the scripts of the 4 cards say so (`place_honor_tokens`); removing is the safety net.
- **Imperial Favor by an effect**: `DiscardImperialFavor` (the favor unclaimed, no side; refused when nobody has it) and `TakeImperialFavor` (`target_player_id`, `side`: from its unclaimed state or from a player, the holder included, to turn it) are special; the claim of the glory count keeps its message (`ClaimImperialFavor`, which moves the game on): an effect is another message. Taking it writes the same entry as the glory count (`ImperialFavorClaimedEvent`). The web client offers both from the badge of the favor in the bar of its holder, and from a dashed badge in the bar of the viewer while nobody has it. The scripts of the 9 cards say so (8 discard it, Fawning Diplomat claims it); Bayushi Shoju ("your opponents cannot have the Imperial Favor") is left to the players.
- **Cards into provinces**: `FillProvince` takes `face_up` and fills a province whatever lies on it (Rally, City of the Rich Frog filled to 3 by three fills, refilling face up: A Season of War, Cycle of Rebirth, Bustling Academy, Isawa Eju, Windswept Yurt, sent before the end of the resolution, so that the engine finds the province filled and does not refill it face down); still not the stronghold province (no card fills it). `PutCardIntoProvince` (special, `card_id`, `province_id`, `face_up`) moves a dynasty card of its owner, from the dynasty deck (a card looked at), the dynasty discard pile, another province or play (the rules of leaving play apply: fate and status token returned, attachments discarded, out of the conflict), into one of their provinces, the stronghold province included (Recalled Defenses, Mountaintop Statuary), broken or not; the province it leaves is refilled once the effect is resolved, if still empty (Logistics), and the deck is not shuffled (`shuffle_dynasty_deck`). A holding moved face up keeps its honor tokens, face down it returns them. `PutHoldingIntoProvince` stays (a holding of the discard pile, face up: the scripts of Rebuild and its kind). The scripts: the 27 Rally cards say `fill_province`, the 17 cards of the category say `put_card_into_province` (Educated Heimin and The Wealth of the Crane no longer as `fill_province`); 5 Rally cards got their first script. The web client offers both fills on every regular province, the form of `put_card_into_province` (a province, face up by default) on the dynasty cards of the provinces, the dynasty discard pile and in play, and in the window of the cards looked at, two buttons per province (face up, face down) for a card of the dynasty deck. No mockup for those: forms and buttons of the existing panels. A province holding several cards shows the depth of the others behind the card on top and a badge with their number, which opens their list (mockup: screen 10 of `GiPbGSPNcvLD2r9eFKKNp5`, validated): the cards put onto the stronghold province are listed there, the stronghold staying on top.
- **What is being resolved**: the engine records the card of each event played and ability triggered (`PlayerState::resolutions`, with its timing), so that the web client reminds the player of the effect they apply before `EndResolution` (mockup: screen 9 of `GiPbGSPNcvLD2r9eFKKNp5`). The opponent and the spectators read it in a line of the bar (no mockup: the card named, its text on hover), and the card each player resolves is marked on the board. Characters and attachments played are not recorded: entering play needs no reminder, and the few cards reacting to it are triggered as reactions, recorded then. The engine does not know which ability of the card is used: the client emphasizes the paragraphs of the timing. A resolution counter of a game in progress before this change is lost (the field is gone): the player can pass at once.
- **Conflict opportunities by an effect**: two messages rather than one with an `immediately` flag, since the cards declaring the next conflict at once (Breakthrough, Ivory Kingdoms Unicorn) give no additional conflict. `GrantConflictOpportunity` (special, `target_player_id`, `type`): one more opportunity and one more conflict of that type, recorded apart from the declared types (`PlayerState::additionalConflictTypes`) so that an additional military conflict granted before the first one (Shori, at the beginning of the phase) still allows two; reset at the end of the round. `DeclareNextConflict` (special, `target_player_id`): the target, who must have an opportunity left, declares the next conflict with one of their own (`GameState::nextConflictPlayerId`, read by `DeclareConflictEnterListener`), the order going on after them; Ivory Kingdoms Unicorn then switches the type of the conflict declared (`switch_conflict_type`). The view tells the types a player may still declare (`declarable_conflict_types`), which the web client reads for the declaration and the icons of the bar; a click on these icons offers both effects (behind the "+": special). The scripts of the 8 cards say so (Shori got its first script). No mockup: buttons of an existing popover.
- **Playing from elsewhere than the hand**: the play messages accept a card of any player from where a card of its class is played (`PlayService`): a conflict card of a hand, a conflict deck or a conflict discard pile, a dynasty character face up in a province, of a dynasty deck or a dynasty discard pile, a card set aside (removed from the game, under a card); not the effect allowing it, as for every card. Any card of a deck, not only its top: the client knows it once looked at (Kyuden Hida plays one of the top three). `PlayCharacterFromHand` became `PlayCharacter` (`play_character`, the name of the MCP tool too); `PlayCharacterFromProvince` stays, the play of the dynasty phase (a different step, a different message). The sender controls what they play (Kunshu, Infiltrator: `take_control` once in play), an event goes to the discard pile of its owner, back on top when played from it (`play_event`, a transition of its own: `discard_card` would log a discard). The events are available whatever the hand holds (`play_interrupt_event` and `play_reaction_event` are always listed, `play_action_event` with the opportunity to act): reading the decks would tell the players what a secret deck holds. The web client offers the plays on the cards of the discard piles and, outside the dynasty phase, on the characters of the provinces (`playActions()`), and on the cards of a deck looked at, in the window of the cards looked at, with the form of the card opened (`PlayForm.vue`). No mockup: buttons and forms of the existing panels.
- **Cards set aside** (`RemoveFromGame`, `PlaceCardUnder`, special; categories 2 and 3 below): two stack types out of play, created when a card first goes there, so that the games in progress needed nothing (`SetAsideStacks`): the cards removed from the game of each player, the cards under a card (its owner's stack, `Stack::host`, its foreign key removed with the card). Decided against the first proposal: a card removed face down goes to the pile of the sender, not of its owner, and only the sender knows it (with "its owner and the sender", Stolen Secrets would show the stolen card to its victim); a card face down under a card is known to the owner of that card, whoever sent it (Under Siege: the defender knows their hand set aside). The cards under a character or a holding are discarded once it leaves its place (`HostLeaveListener`), not when it is bowed, changes control or province; a province and a stronghold never leave theirs. A host is a card in play, a dynasty card face up in a province, a province or a stronghold, never the card itself. From there, every message taking a card of a hand or a discard pile takes them (play, put into play, into a province, into a hand, on a deck, discard); the web client offers it in the card opened and in the window of the cards looked at (only the cards of the viewer as hosts there: Kaiu Shihobu, Daidoji Uji).
- **Worker**: the tasks done out of the requests (the house bot, ending the games every player has left) are messages of `src/Task` on a bus of their own (`task.bus`, none of the middlewares of the actions, whose transaction would hold the lock of a game for the whole task), consumed by one worker (`messenger:consume async`, a service of compose from the image of the application) from the Doctrine transport already configured (`LISTEN`/`NOTIFY`: no Redis nor RabbitMQ). One worker only for now, which the scheduler will need (one consumer of its transport). The house bot moved there from `kernel.terminate`: its turns survive restarts and no longer hold a thread of FrankenPHP; reading a game no longer wakes it (a turn is not lost any more, and the web client reads a game every 15 seconds: one task each time). `default.bus` is the bus of the actions rather than a bus by default: to rename (`action.bus`) some day.
- **Abandoned games**: the player left alone ends the game, which they win (`ClaimAbandonment`, victory condition `abandonment`, the absent player eliminated like a concession), once the game has waited for the opponent only for 3 minutes without changing, or for 1 minute once the opponent no longer reads it (not seen for 60 seconds: `Player::lastSeenAt`, written by every read of a player, at most every 10 seconds); nothing ends the game but them, so they may wait longer. Whom the game waits for is read from the state (`AwaitedPlayersResolver`): the players to whom an action moving the game on is available (pass, end the resolution, bid, declare a conflict, defenders, a total, a statistic, end a duel…: their handlers say so), plus the two steps that end once cards are face up; the effects on the board, available to everybody, move nothing on. The time is checked before the message is sent (`AbandonmentClaimer`, `POST /api/games/{id}/abandonment`, the MCP tool `end_abandoned_game`), not by the handler, which only checks the state: the events replay. The prompt of the web client is read apart (`GET /api/games/{id}/abandonment`, never stored), not in the view, whose ETag is its version. "Wait" asks again 3 minutes later. No mockup: a box in the panel of actions.
- **Games every player has left** (both players gone, or the human gone in a game of the house bot): the platform ends them without a winner (`EndAbandonedGame`, every player eliminated as abandoned, `winnerId` null, victory condition `abandonment`; the web client shows "No winner"), once unchanged and read by none of their players for an hour (`AbandonedGamesEnder::LEFT_AFTER`); the house bot never reads a game, so only its opponent counts. Rather than the player the game waited for losing: both have left, and the one not awaited could have claimed. The house bot does not claim (a human pausing would lose to an automatic claim). Checked every 5 minutes by a task of the Symfony Scheduler (`App\Schedule`, stateful), consumed by the same worker as `async` with `--bus=task.bus`, the scheduler setting no bus on its messages. A player reading the game between the query and the message is not seen (the version only guards against a change of the game): a race of a few milliseconds after an hour away.
- **Turning facedown**: `TurnProvinceFacedown` (special) on an unbroken face-up province, of any player; the province keeps its id (the players have seen it, it stays where it is), its cards and its tokens. The Eminent keyword is left to the players, like every card rule (and the Eminent provinces do not start the game face up either: not modeled). No `TurnDynastyCardFacedown`: a new id for a card turned face down in place would hide nothing (the opponent sees the new id where the card was, then follows it), and Governor's Spy, the only card turning dynasty cards face down, is `RedistributeProvinceCards` (special): the cards of the provinces put back face down, each into the province chosen, all with new ids and without a transition per card, so that the log does not tell where each went (a pile set aside then shuffled was considered: a log that speaks of cards removed from the game, and a dozen messages). The sender sees the cards first with `LookAtCards` on the provinces (`HiddenPile::Provinces`, an entry per province), which opens the window of the redistribution (mockup: screens 5 and 6 of `LdtL4NT1SnDLTzRZpz376x`), from the form in the "+" of the card that needs it; "each province ends up with at least one card" is a warning of the window, not a check. The scripts: the 5 cards turning a province face down say `turn_province_facedown`, Governor's Spy `look_at_cards` and `redistribute_province_cards` (no longer `switch_province_cards` nor `put_card_into_province`).
- **The house bot** is Kyūden no Karō, "the steward of the palace": a title rather than a name, being a bot. It is faced from the queue (the deck chosen there), and named with the label "Bot" only in the games.

## Known limitations

- **Rules not enforced**: uniqueness, mulligan, the Limited keyword and "max X per conflict / round", covert (the keyword and the number of evaded characters), ancestral attachments (discarded with their character). The end of a lasting effect is not handled: a player takes control back with `TakeControl` (Blackmail).
- **Spectators**: the list of the games has no round number (nor has the board); a spectator sees the same layouts as the player at the bottom (the declaration of a conflict folds their hand and provinces), and a few texts of the board still speak to the player; the players are told who watches but cannot refuse it.
- **Web client**: the province choices of a character in play are those of its controller (the view does not tell the owner: a character whose control was taken is refused); covert is not offered (`evaded_ids` sent empty); a multi-province conflict shows its first province only; no round number; no layout of its own for the draw and fate phases, the duels and the reaction windows.
- **House bot**: plays no conflict card and triggers no ability, always puts the Imperial Favor on its military side, never discards from its provinces, never ends a duel. It plays in the worker (`PlayHouseBotTurn`); while the worker is stopped, its opponent waits (no turn is played elsewhere); a failure makes it pass, else concede, the reason in the event for the developers only.
- **Agents (MCP)**: no game played by an agent yet; an agent that stops without conceding is ended by its opponent (`end_abandoned_game` for an agent left alone), or by the platform after an hour when both have left; the view is sent whole on each `get_game`; check that clients keep the whole `instructions` (about 9 kB).
- **Scaling**: each long-poll holds a FrankenPHP thread for up to 30 s (classic mode, 20 threads); the OAuth state, the rate limiter and the MCP sessions live in filesystem caches (one host only; MCP sessions lost on restart).
- **Security**: a Mercure subscriber token outlives a revoked session by up to an hour; no rate limit on creating guests and accounts; deleting an account asks no re-authentication (Symfony 8.2 brings one).
- **Decks of EmeraldDB**: the decks of the Emerald Legacy card pool are refused (their new cards are unknown to the engine); a deck whose page EmeraldDB does not show (private, not published) cannot be downloaded: only what `/api/decklists/<id>` answers without authentication.
- **Data**: card images point at FFG's CloudFront name (`d2qmv0gg5fizll.cloudfront.net`: the FFG CDN name has a mismatched certificate), which may change; the banned and restricted lists are not checked, and copies are counted by title where EmeraldDB counts by version.
- `npm audit`: 4 high vulnerabilities in the lint tooling (`braces`), not shipped; the fix is a breaking downgrade, wait for an update.

"Mental" effects (skills, costs, strength, glory, keywords, targeting restrictions, cancelling an effect, limits) are not gaps: the players declare the values and do not apply what is cancelled. ### Messages proposed

In order of value (cards covered, cost). Like the other effect messages: accepted in any place, from any player, on any card or player; each writes its entry in the log.

1. **Rings**: `PlaceFate` / `RemoveFate` on a ring (an optional `ring` instead of `card_id`); **`ReturnRing`** (`ring`): a claimed or contested ring to the unclaimed pool, with its fate (reverses the "not planned"); `ClaimRing` accepted on a ring claimed by another player (Roving Michibiku).
2. **`PutAttachmentIntoPlay`** (`card_id`, `target_card_id`, `controller_id`): from a hand, a deck or a discard pile, without cost nor opportunity; and `PutCharacterIntoPlay` accepting the dynasty deck (a card known through `look_at_cards`). `PutHoldingIntoProvince` accepting the dynasty deck (Frontline Engineer).
3. **`LookAtCards` / `RevealCards`** with `random: true` on a hand: the cards drawn by the generator of the game (category 11).
4. **Conflicts**: **`AddAttackedProvince`** (`province_id`) and **`MoveConflict`** (`province_id`: the new attacked province, revealed by `RevealProvince`); **`RestoreProvince`** (`province_id`: a broken province face up again, unless the game has ended); the stronghold province check of `DeclareConflict` lifted by an `ignore_stronghold_restriction` flag (Scouted Terrain).
5. **`GiveActionOpportunity`** (`target_player_id`): in an action window, the target becomes the active player, passes kept (Child of the Plains; Hawk Tattoo, Current of the Beryt and Austere Exemplar: the sender gives it back to themselves after `EndResolution`).
6. **`CancelDuel`**: the duel ends at any step, no result (Stay Your Hand).
7. **Characters as attachments**: a new place of the conflict and dynasty character workflows (`attached`), with **`PlayCharacterAsAttachment`** (`card_id`, `target_card_id`, `card_cost`) and **`DetachAsCharacter`** (`card_id`): category 5, the most work, last.
