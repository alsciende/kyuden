# HANDOFF

## Current status

Every phase of the round is modeled: a game loops from round to round until a victory condition is met. The engine is a board (see README): the players apply the cards with generic effect messages, which now cover the cards of the Core Set but those listed under "Next steps". Around it: the HTTP API, the MCP server for bots, the house bot, accounts (guests, password, linked providers), the queue, friendly games (own bot or a friend), spectators (an account watches any game in progress), the web client and the production stack (FrankenPHP, CI, releases on `ghcr.io`), deployed at kyuden.app (DigitalOcean) by the release of each tag `vX.Y.Z` of `main`.

## Principles to keep

- **The engine moves pieces, the players apply the cards.** Effect messages (bow, honor, fate, discard, move…) are accepted in any place, from any player, on any card: about 30 cards make the opponent act during the player's action, and most effects target the opponent's cards. Values that cards modify (skills, province strength, glory, dueling statistic, costs) are declared by the players, prefilled by the clients; the engine applies the rules to them. It does not check triggering conditions, timings of the abilities of cards (cards gain and lose abilities), limits per round, attachment restrictions, nor that a ring effect matches the ring.
- **Randomness and visibility** are the only places where the engine does more than move pieces: every draw goes through the generator of the game (replay), and what a player sees depends only on the state, secrets stored apart (`pendingHonorBid` / `honorBid`).
- **A card gets a new id when the players lose track of it** (a shuffle), never otherwise: a card returned to hand or put on a deck without a shuffle stays known, as at a real table.
- **No backward compatibility of the event log**: messages were renamed several times (`EndResolution`, `PlayActionEvent`…), and older events cannot be replayed. Acceptable while no game is live; to reconsider once one is.
- **Interrupts and reactions** are accepted from any player in any place and ended with `EndResolution`; the interrupt and reaction windows of the rules (first player first, consecutive passes) are not modeled. The `react_to_*` places of the conflict are generous on purpose: to lock down once the game has been tested.
- **Accounts**: no email nor personal data; an account is recovered through the providers it is linked to, which the site should press players to link. Only a bot gets API keys, so that a program is always known as a bot; a human helped by an AI is a matter of the terms of use. The `playerId` is public, never a credential.
- **Mercure announces, the clients read**: the hub carries the version of the game, not its state, which would have to be published per player (secrets), kept in the history of the hub, and computed for every message; the delays of reading the game again are not felt.
- **Mockups first** for the screens of the web client: the board and the cards looked at (Claude artifact `GiPbGSPNcvLD2r9eFKKNp5`), the accounts, the queue, the friendly games and the spectators (`LBUBwNBPdSopj5NMj5GLTL`, row "Watch a game in progress").
- **Spectators**: an account (not a guest nor a bot: the list of the spectators names accounts) watches any game in progress, its own included, and sees only what is public (`Visibility` with no viewer). Being a spectator is not part of the game: no version, no log, no event; it is kept apart (`Spectator`, `SpectatorRegistry`), a spectator who stops reading for 45 seconds forgotten, and each arrival or departure announced (`spectators_updated`). The client shows a spectator the board of a player (the first in player order, then the other one), with their names instead of "You" and "Opp.". The end of a game is told to a spectator by the log only.
- **Special actions and card scripts**: the effect messages few cards need are marked `special` (`#[AppAction(special: true)]`: 30 of them, those that react to or undo an effect of the opponent stay basic, `TakeControl` and `TransferHonor` included) and offered by the web client behind a "+" (the card dialog, the popovers of the bars, the window of a ring), those the active cards of the player need first, every other one on demand ("Show every action", the safety net for what the engine does not know). Which card needs what is said by the script of the card (`src/CardScript`, a class per card overriding `AbstractCardScript`), not by the fixtures, so that a card can later say more than a list (conditions, skill bonuses, triggering conditions). A script informs, it never decides. The first 241 scripts were generated from a review of the texts (special actions only): approximations accepted where no message fits exactly (Calling in Favors, Karada District and Shinjo Saddle as `move_attachment`; Frontline Engineer and Kaiu Shihobu as `put_holding_into_province`; Educated Heimin and The Wealth of the Crane as `fill_province`; Roving Michibiku as `claim_ring`; fate from the general pool onto a ring as `move_fate`), and the effects without a message (see "Cards not playable yet") left out. The timings of the triggered abilities and whether they are granted to the card attached to moved from the card fixtures (and `LogicalCard`) to the scripts too, generated by the maker from the former fixtures (`--timing`, `--granted-to-attached`): 899 scripts, a card with any of the three.
- **Status tokens**: placing, discarding or moving a token is not honoring nor dishonoring (glossary, "Personal Honor, Personal Dishonor"): `PlaceStatusToken`, `DiscardStatusToken` and `MoveStatusToken` (special) write their own entries, not `PersonalHonorChangedEvent`. `PlaceStatusToken` (`token`: `honored` or `dishonored`, the enum `StatusToken`) covers a character receiving a token without being honored (Final Whisper) and the dishonored status token of a province (Overrun, Return from Shadows, Meticulous Scout: its printed text treated as blank by the players). A province holds a status token like a character (`CardState::personalHonor`, shown on the card): placed and discarded (Untainted, Perfect Land Ethos), not moved (no card does). The engine does not check which kind goes on a province: an honored token is accepted there too, the players' business. The same rules everywhere: a token placed or moved onto a card with the other token discards both; onto a card with a token of its kind, it is refused ("cannot become honored"), rather than lost. The scripts of the 33 cards that place, discard or move one say so, the Disguised characters included (they inherit the token of the character they replace: `MoveStatusToken`, after playing them). Status tokens and honor tokens are different things (see below); the tainted status tokens are not modeled (no card of the fixtures uses them).
- **Honor tokens** on provinces and holdings are counters, like fate, not status tokens: `PlaceHonorTokens` and `RemoveHonorTokens` (special, `card_id`, `amount`), on a province (any, face down included) or a holding face up in a province, of any player; the honor of the players never changes (Distinguished Dojo gains honor by sacrificing itself: `GainHonor`, then `SacrificeCard`). A holding is in play while face up in a province: leaving it, it returns its tokens to the pool (logged), switched to another province it keeps them; a province keeps them, broken included (Public Forum not being broken instead remains a gap: "Conflicts" below). No move (no card does). The card shows their number (a vermilion badge); the scripts of the 4 cards say so (`place_honor_tokens`); removing is the safety net.
- **Imperial Favor by an effect**: `DiscardImperialFavor` (the favor unclaimed, no side; refused when nobody has it) and `TakeImperialFavor` (`target_player_id`, `side`: from its unclaimed state or from a player, the holder included, to turn it) are special; the claim of the glory count keeps its message (`ClaimImperialFavor`, which moves the game on): an effect is another message. Taking it writes the same entry as the glory count (`ImperialFavorClaimedEvent`). The web client offers both from the badge of the favor in the bar of its holder, and from a dashed badge in the bar of the viewer while nobody has it. The scripts of the 9 cards say so (8 discard it, Fawning Diplomat claims it); Bayushi Shoju ("your opponents cannot have the Imperial Favor") is left to the players.
- **What is being resolved**: the engine records the card of each event played and ability triggered (`PlayerState::resolutions`, with its timing), so that the web client reminds the player of the effect they apply before `EndResolution` (mockup: screen 9 of `GiPbGSPNcvLD2r9eFKKNp5`). The opponent and the spectators read it in a line of the bar (no mockup: the card named, its text on hover), and the card each player resolves is marked on the board. Characters and attachments played are not recorded: entering play needs no reminder, and the few cards reacting to it are triggered as reactions, recorded then. The engine does not know which ability of the card is used: the client emphasizes the paragraphs of the timing. A resolution counter of a game in progress before this change is lost (the field is gone): the player can pass at once.
- **The house bot** is Kyūden no Karō, "the steward of the palace": a title rather than a name, being a bot. It is faced from the queue (the deck chosen there), and named with the label "Bot" only in the games.

## Known limitations

- **Rules not enforced**: uniqueness, mulligan, the Limited keyword and "max X per conflict / round", covert (the keyword and the number of evaded characters), ancestral attachments (discarded with their character). The end of a lasting effect is not handled: a player takes control back with `TakeControl` (Blackmail).
- **Spectators**: the list of the games has no round number (nor has the board); a spectator sees the same layouts as the player at the bottom (the declaration of a conflict folds their hand and provinces), and a few texts of the board still speak to the player; the players are told who watches but cannot refuse it.
- **Web client**: covert is not offered (`evaded_ids` sent empty); a multi-province conflict shows its first province only; no round number; no layout of its own for the draw and fate phases, the duels and the reaction windows.
- **House bot**: plays no conflict card and triggers no ability, always puts the Imperial Favor on its military side, never discards from its provinces, never ends a duel. A turn it has not played (its request ended before: a restart, a timeout) is played when its opponent reads the game again (the web client reads it again after 15 seconds without a change); a failure makes it pass, else concede, the reason in the event for the developers only. Kept in `kernel.terminate` rather than a Messenger worker: a worker would free the FrankenPHP threads and keep the turns across restarts, not worth a process to deploy while the games against the bot are few.
- **Agents (MCP)**: no game played by an agent yet; an agent that stops without conceding blocks the game (no timeout); the view is sent whole on each `get_game`; check that clients keep the whole `instructions` (about 9 kB).
- **Scaling**: each long-poll holds a FrankenPHP thread for up to 30 s (classic mode, 20 threads); the OAuth state, the rate limiter and the MCP sessions live in filesystem caches (one host only; MCP sessions lost on restart).
- **Security**: a Mercure subscriber token outlives a revoked session by up to an hour; no rate limit on creating guests and accounts; deleting an account asks no re-authentication (Symfony 8.2 brings one).
- **Decks of EmeraldDB**: the decks of the Emerald Legacy card pool are refused (their new cards are unknown to the engine); a deck whose page EmeraldDB does not show (private, not published) cannot be downloaded: only what `/api/decklists/<id>` answers without authentication.
- **Data**: card images point at FFG's CloudFront name (`d2qmv0gg5fizll.cloudfront.net`: the FFG CDN name has a mismatched certificate), which may change; the banned and restricted lists are not checked, and copies are counted by title where EmeraldDB counts by version.
- `npm audit`: 4 high vulnerabilities in the lint tooling (`braces`), not shipped; the fix is a breaking downgrade, wait for an update.

## Next steps

### Cards not playable yet

A review of the texts of every card of the fixtures (1,129 cards, all of FFG) against the public messages: about 150 cards have an effect that the players cannot apply. "Mental" effects (skills, costs, strength, glory, keywords, targeting restrictions, cancelling an effect, limits) are not gaps: the players declare the values and do not apply what is cancelled. By category of the missing effect, with a few examples each:

1. **Turning facedown**: a province (Kitsuki Yaruma, Saadiyah al-Mozedu, Traveling Philosopher, Untamed Steppe, Hidden Mountain Pass), the dynasty cards of the provinces (Governor's Spy).
2. **Removing from the game**: Stolen Secrets (facedown, playable afterwards), Kyuden Isawa, Isawa Ujina, Karmic Balance, Kitsuki Kagi, Ethereal Alignment, Bayushi Kachiko, Isawa Tadaka, Dragon Tattoo, Champions of Yomi, Shadow Step (and back into play), Trading on the Sand Road.
3. **Cards under a card, set aside**: Back-Alley Hideaway (a character facedown on the holding), Kaiu Shihobu (holdings under the stronghold province), Daidoji Uji (4 cards under him, playable), Under Siege (the hand set aside, then back).
4. **Playing a card from elsewhere than the hand** (the Play messages take a card of the hand, and the characters of the provinces in their step only):
   - the conflict discard pile: Guidance of the Ancestors, Kyuden Isawa, Togashi Mitsu, Warm Welcome, Right Hand of the Emperor, Exposed Courtyard, In Service to My Lord, Dragon Tattoo; the opponent's: Bayushi Kachiko, Kunshu;

   - out of play: Stolen Secrets, Trading on the Sand Road (the cards of both players), Back-Alley Hideaway, Daidoji Uji (37);
   - a province outside the dynasty step: Sneaky Shinjo, Hidden Moon Dojo, Prepared Ambush, Wind's Path, Daidoji Uji (18).
5. **Characters as attachments, and back**: Tattooed Wanderer, Togashi Kazue, Ancient Master, Serene Ise Zumi, Togashi Acolyte, Promising Youth, Voice of the Ancestors (a character of the dynasty discard pile); an attachment that becomes a character: Togashi Hoshi, Promising Youth.

7. **Cards into provinces** (`SwitchProvinceCards` swaps, `FillProvince` puts the top card facedown into an empty province only):
   - adding a card to a province that holds one, the Rally keyword (26 cards: Akodo Reserve Company, Butcher of the Fallen, Daidoji Marketplace, Ikoma Tsanuri…) and City of the Rich Frog (filled to 3);
   - from the dynasty deck, face up: Walking the Way, Our Foe Does Not Wait, The Wealth of the Crane, Breaking In, The Western Wind, Emperor's Summons, Unyielding Sensei, Miya Library, Kaiu Forges;
   - from the dynasty discard pile or from under the stronghold province: Miya Satoshi, Kaiu Shihobu;
   - from a province to another without a swap: Mountaintop Statuary, Recalled Defenses, Logistics, Soshi Diviner, Governor's Spy (the cards redistributed);
   - a character leaving play into a province: Master of Many Lifetimes, The Receding Tide;
   - control of a holding moved into a province of the player: Akodo Zentaro.
8. **Rings**:
    - fate from the general token pool onto rings, or back (the workaround `GainFate` + `MoveFate` logs a gain of fate): Written in the Stars, Army of the Rising Wave, Fuchi Mura, Taoist Adept;
    - returning a claimed ring to the unclaimed pool (`SwitchRing` claims another ring in its place): Severed from the Stream (every ring), Deduction, Perceptive Kitsuki, Overlooked Community; the contested ring returned instead of claimed: Henshin Mysteries;
    - taking a ring of the claimed pool of another player: Roving Michibiku;
    - a ring out of the game until the end of the round: Sudden Tempest; an attachment on a ring: Greater Understanding.
9. **Conflicts**:
    - an additional conflict: Hisu Mori Tōride (both), Hida O-Ushi, Suffer the Consequences, Shori, Courteous Scheming;
    - declaring the next conflict at once, out of turn: Breakthrough, Ivory Kingdoms Unicorn;
    - moving the conflict to another province or adding one: Chasing the Sun, Talisman of the Sun, Matsu Agetoki, Know the Terrain, Shrug Off Despair, Golden Plains, A War on Two Fronts (`ConflictState::attackedProvinceIds` is ready);
    - attacking the stronghold province before 3 provinces are broken: Scouted Terrain;
    - a broken province restored, or not broken: Ethereal Alignment, Public Forum, Akodo Toshiro;
    - the defenders declared before the attackers: Seven Stings Keep;
    - the conflict resolved as if both players had passed: Moment of Perfect Beauty.
10. **Opportunities to act**: the attacking player acting first in a conflict (Child of the Plains); taking another action after one (Hawk Tattoo), or several actions (Current of the Beryt, Austere Exemplar). Several cards played within one action (Wayfarer's Camp, Invoke the Divine) already work: an active player who has acted can still play.
11. **Random cards of a hand, looked at or revealed** (`LookAtCards` / `RevealCards` take the whole hand): Shrewd Investigator, Kitsuki Chiari, Isawa Tadaka, Court of Justice, Overhear.
12. **What the engine applies and a card prevents or changes**: cancelling a duel before its result (Stay Your Hand: `EndDuel` only once it is resolved); the honor loss of an unopposed conflict (Ikebana Artisan); losing the last honor (Duty: the game ends at once); triggering the ability of a card one does not control (Kujira's Hireling: `AbilityService` checks the controller); a secret honor dial outside a bid (Maze of Illusion).
13. **Phases**: skipping or repeating one: A Season of War (a second dynasty phase), Jurojin's Curse (a second fate phase), Trading on the Sand Road (no draw phase).

Borderline, not counted: "does not bow as a result of conflict resolution" (17 cards such as Indomitable Will, Sashimono: `ReadyCard` afterwards, which can trigger what reacts to readying); the hand put at the bottom of the deck in a random order (An Ocean in a Drop: the player chooses the order); a Disguised character played over another (`PlayCharacterFromHand`, then `MoveAttachment`, `MoveFate` and `DiscardCard` of the character replaced, which then leaves play).

### Messages proposed

In order of value (cards covered, cost). Like the other effect messages: accepted in any place, from any player, on any card or player; each writes its entry in the log.

1. **`FillProvince`** with `face_up` and adding to a province that holds cards (Rally, City of the Rich Frog, A Season of War, Cycle of Rebirth), and **`PutCardIntoProvince`** (`card_id`, `province_id`, `face_up`): a dynasty card from the dynasty deck (known through `look_at_cards`), the dynasty discard pile, another province, out of play, or a character in play (it leaves play), into a province of its owner. Covers category 7 but control (Akodo Zentaro).
2. **`GrantConflictOpportunity`** (`target_player_id`, `type`, `immediately`): one more conflict of that type (`PlayerState::conflictOpportunities` and `declaredConflictTypes`); `immediately` makes the target the next to declare (`DeclareConflictEnterListener`: a flag of `GameState` read before the order), which covers Breakthrough and Ivory Kingdoms Unicorn.
3. **Playing from elsewhere than the hand**: `PlayActionEvent`, `PlayInterruptEvent`, `PlayReactionEvent`, `PlayAttachment` and `PlayCharacterFromHand` accept a card of a discard pile, the top of a deck, out of play (see 4) or face up in a province, of any player (`HandService::assertPlayable()` and the client: a card played is offered from where it is); the sender controls what they play, which goes to the discard pile of its owner. `PlayCharacterFromHand` from a province covers the characters played from provinces during a conflict. Same opportunity and resolution as from the hand. Category 4.
4. **`RemoveFromGame`** (`card_id`, `face_down`): a new stack of each player (`StackType::RemovedFromGame`), public unless facedown (its owner and the sender see it then, `Visibility`); `PutCharacterIntoPlay` and the plays of 4 accept its cards (Shadow Step, Stolen Secrets). **`PlaceCardUnder`** (`card_id`, `host_card_id`, `face_down`): a stack of the host card, like the attachments, out of play, discarded with its host (Back-Alley Hideaway, Kaiu Shihobu, Daidoji Uji); Under Siege as `PlaceCardUnder` of each card on the stronghold, then `AddCardToHand` from there.
5. **`TurnProvinceFacedown`** (`province_id`, unbroken, not eminent) and **`TurnDynastyCardFacedown`** (`card_id`, a new id for the card, as a shuffle, so that it cannot be followed: Governor's Spy then redistributes with `PutCardIntoProvince`).
6. **Rings**: `PlaceFate` / `RemoveFate` on a ring (an optional `ring` instead of `card_id`); **`ReturnRing`** (`ring`): a claimed or contested ring to the unclaimed pool, with its fate (reverses the "not planned"); `ClaimRing` accepted on a ring claimed by another player (Roving Michibiku).
7. **`PutAttachmentIntoPlay`** (`card_id`, `target_card_id`, `controller_id`): from a hand, a deck or a discard pile, without cost nor opportunity; and `PutCharacterIntoPlay` accepting the dynasty deck (a card known through `look_at_cards`) and out of play. `PutHoldingIntoProvince` accepting the dynasty deck and the cards under the stronghold province (Frontline Engineer, Kaiu Shihobu).
8. **`LookAtCards` / `RevealCards`** with `random: true` on a hand: the cards drawn by the generator of the game (category 11).
9. **Conflicts**: **`AddAttackedProvince`** (`province_id`) and **`MoveConflict`** (`province_id`: the new attacked province, revealed by `RevealProvince`); **`RestoreProvince`** (`province_id`: a broken province face up again, unless the game has ended); the stronghold province check of `DeclareConflict` lifted by an `ignore_stronghold_restriction` flag (Scouted Terrain).
10. **`GiveActionOpportunity`** (`target_player_id`): in an action window, the target becomes the active player, passes kept (Child of the Plains; Hawk Tattoo, Current of the Beryt and Austere Exemplar: the sender gives it back to themselves after `EndResolution`).
11. **`CancelDuel`**: the duel ends at any step, no result (Stay Your Hand).
12. **Characters as attachments**: a new place of the conflict and dynasty character workflows (`attached`), with **`PlayCharacterAsAttachment`** (`card_id`, `target_card_id`, `card_cost`) and **`DetachAsCharacter`** (`card_id`): category 5, the most work, last.

Not planned, since the cards are few and the changes deep: Seven Stings Keep (the order of the declarations), Moment of Perfect Beauty (the window closed after one opportunity), the phases skipped or repeated (A Season of War, Jurojin's Curse, Trading on the Sand Road), Kujira's Hireling (the controller checked by `AbilityService`), Maze of Illusion, Duty and Ikebana Artisan (losses applied by the engine: the honor given back with `GainHonor`, but the game ends at 0 honor), Henshin Mysteries (`ReturnRing` after the claim), Sudden Tempest, Greater Understanding, Force of the River.

- Next hooks of `AbstractCardScript`: the constant skill bonuses, read by both the prefill of the web client (`board.ts`) and the house bot (`BoardReader`), which compute them twice today; the triggering conditions of the reactions and interrupts, to point the player at the cards that can react.

### Web client

- Choosing the stronghold province: the five provinces larger and centered.

### API and platform

- **Agents**: play a game against `agent/play.sh` and fix what it reports; a timeout for an agent that stops; a compact view if the context fills.
- **Feedback**: read the reasons of the concessions (payload of the `ConcedeGame` events): a console command or a listener of `GameConcededEvent`.
- **Decks**: a deck of EmeraldDB is imported from the URL of its page (`EmeraldDbDeckFetcher`: `POST /api/decks` with `url`, `app:deck:import`, and the field of the queue screen, friendly games included: mockup in the row "Play a game: the queue" of `LBUBwNBPdSopj5NMj5GLTL`); importing does not join the queue, the player still clicks. A user deletes their own decks (`DELETE /api/decks/{id}`, soft-deleted once played, restored when imported again; the button on the tiles of the queue: mockups "hovering one of your decks shows Delete", "confirm before deleting a deck", "deck deleted"), and identical decks are found by their content hash (`Deck::contentHash`, not indexed: an index across users when statistics need it). Then: the text export of EmeraldDB, the banned and restricted lists.
- **Authentication**: rate limit the creation of guests and accounts; press the players to link a provider; sessions expiring after a few hours, renewed by the client.
- **Ranking** of the games that are not friendly.
- **Rewinding a game** (`app:game:rewind <game> <version>`): deleting events changes nothing (the state lives in the entities), so replay instead: read the events up to the version, delete the game, replay them with `EventPlayer` in a transaction. Caveats: a deck or an engine changed since makes the replay diverge; the house bot must not play during it; the clients get a notification per message.
