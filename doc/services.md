# Services

The services around the engine: the house bot, the replay, the trophies, the worker, matchmaking, spectators, notifications, security and Mercure.

- House bot (`Service/Bot`): the bot of the platform, Kyūden no Karō (user `HOUSE_BOT_ID`, `kyuden-bot` in the fixtures, owned by the user `kyuden`; `app:house-bot:create` creates both when missing, and renames the bot when its name is not `HouseBotCreateCommand::NAME`) plays the games of `POST /api/bot-games`. It plays in the worker: once a message has been handled (after the commit), `HouseBotMiddleware` (first of the bus) wakes it (`HouseBot::wake()`), which sends its turn to the worker (the task `Task/PlayHouseBotTurn`) when it plays the game, which has not ended, every message its own (none skipped as a duplicate: the worker may already have played the turn of the first message of a request), except those the bot sends itself; the worker plays it at once (`LISTEN`/`NOTIFY`), through the bus like any player, until it has nothing to do (at most 100 actions per turn): before each message it clears the entity manager, so that `GameLockMiddleware` loads the game again, and sends the version it decided on (`ExpectedVersionStamp`): a stale version makes it decide again. Its opponent never waits for it in vain: a turn waits in the transport while the worker is stopped, and a worker killed while playing it plays it again; a failure (a bug of the strategy, an action the engine refuses) is logged, then the bot does nothing in the step if it can (pass the opportunity, end the resolution, pass the conflict, declare no defenders), else concedes (`ConcedeGame`, its reason the failure: the players are not told why). The player sees its moves through the notifications of the game. `BotStrategy` chooses the action from the available ones, reading the board through `BoardReader` (the current skills, glory, dueling statistics and province strengths, computed from the cards like the web client prefills them; only what a player sees: a face-down province of the opponent has an assumed strength): it plays the characters of its provinces (the most expensive it can afford, with the fate it will not spend), declares the conflicts it expects to win as if the opponent defended with every ready character (breaking a province first, with the fewest attackers), defends to win or to keep its province, resolves the effect of the ring it wins (`RingEffectChooser`, which reads its own messages since the step began in the events of the game), bids in duels and declares its statistic, and does what the framework steps require (stronghold province, keeping its cards should a game of its own have a mulligan, revealing its facedown dynasty cards and the province it defends, fate of its stronghold, honor bid of 1, the totals, the Imperial Favor on its military side). It plays no conflict card, triggers no ability and ends no duel; it passes every other opportunity. `app:bot:play <game>` makes it play in a game.
- Replay (`Service/Replay/GameReplayer`): a game that has ended is rebuilt from its events for one of its players, on a copy under another id: the events are dispatched again (`EventPlayer`), their payloads translated (`Model/ReplayIdTranslator`: the id of the game becomes that of the copy, every other id has the salt of the original swapped for that of the copy, so that it names what the copy generated from the same seed; the user and the deck of a player joining are kept). Once created, the copy is marked as the replay of the original for the user (`Game::markAsReplay()`, one replay per user and game: starting again deletes the previous one, `GameRepository::delete()`, in SQL), and kept between two steps (`find()`, `getStep()`). `proceed()` replays the next event whoever sends it (the action the replay stopped before), then the events up to the next one the player sent (a public message), excluded, or to the end; or up to a version given (`Model/ReplayStep`: the versions, the next event of the player, whether it has ended). The messages carry a `ReplayStamp` (`Middleware/Stamp`), so that nobody is notified (`GameUpdatedNotificationMiddleware`) and the house bot does not play (`HouseBotMiddleware`); `GameReplayMiddleware` rejects every other message on the copy. The copy records its own events, from which its log is read, and is listed nowhere (the queries of the games in progress, of the games of a user, of the abandoned games skip it). An event the engine refuses stops the replay at its version (`ReplayDivergedException`: an engine or a deck changed since, or a game created before its ids were salted); `findDivergence()` compares the log of the copy with that of the original, version by version (keys sorted: `jsonb` does not keep their order), ids translated. The task `Task/DeleteStaleReplays` of the worker, every 5 minutes (`App\Schedule`), deletes the copies their user has not read for an hour (`StaleReplaysDeleter`, `Player::lastSeenAt` of their player in the copy); deleting an account deletes its replays. `app:game:replay <game> --as=<user> [--step]` replays a game in a transaction rolled back (nothing of the copy stays), showing its log as the player reads it, the next action before each stop (waiting for Enter with `--step`), then whether the copy is identical to the game.
- Images of the cards: a WebP per card, named by its code (`cards/<code>.webp`), in the bucket `kyuden-images` of Cloudflare R2, under a version (`v1/`, so that they are cached for a year, `immutable`: an image changed goes under a new version) and served by Cloudflare on `images.kyuden.app` (the zone `kyuden.app` is at Cloudflare, which proxies the site too). The API names them (`CARD_IMAGES_URL`, `https://images.kyuden.app/v1` by default, in development and in the tests too); the images of the printings (`Printing::imageUrl`, from the fixtures) are kept as data, not used. Uploaded with rclone, from a directory out of the repository: `rclone copy <dir>/cards r2:kyuden-images/v1/cards --exclude .DS_Store --header-upload "Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable"`.
- Trophies (`Service/Trophy`; the catalogue and its rules: `TROPHIES.md`): each trophy of `Enum/Trophy` has its rule, a class of `src/Trophy` named after its case (`TrophyInterface`, tagged: `getTrophy()` and `isEarnedBy(Player, Game, GameState)`; the trophies of a clan share `AbstractClanTrophy`), which reads what is stored (the players, `winnerId` and `victoryCondition`, the clans of the strongholds), so that the games played before earn them too; `GameTrophies` asks every rule for each human player of a game that has ended: a game against the house bot (`Game::isTraining()`) played to its end, won or lost (`house_bot_game`, the plain pattern), and a standard game (`Game::isStandard()`: neither friendly nor against the house bot) won with a stronghold of a Great Clan (`clan_<clan>`, its mon). Played to its end means by honor or by breaking the stronghold province, never by a concession or an abandonment (`VictoryCondition::isPlayedToTheEnd()`); only humans earn them (guests included, who keep them by registering), never in a replay. `EventListener/GameWonTrophiesListener` awards them once a game is won (`GameWonEvent`), in the transaction of the message that ends it, so that the end of the game shows them at once; nothing goes in the log of the game. `app:trophies:award [--dry-run]` awards those of every game that has ended, the first ended first, as earned when each ended: run it after a new trophy (a trophy earned stays as it is). To add a trophy earned at the end of a game:

  1. Write it in `TROPHIES.md`: its goal, its exact rule, its reward.
  2. Add its case to `Enum/Trophy` (a value of 32 characters at most, in snake case; the order of the cases is that of the catalogue on `/trophies`) and its reward to `getReward()`: a mon or a pattern of the nobori (a new one is a case of `Enum/Mon` or `Enum/BannerPattern`, its asset and `front/src/data/nobori.ts`, a mockup first); a trophy without a reward makes `getReward()` nullable, and `TrophyCase::award()`, `TrophyViewFactory` and the tiles of `/trophies` with it.
  3. Write its rule: a class of `src/Trophy` named after the case (`TrophyInterface`, registered by its tag; `AbstractClanTrophy` for a clan), whose `isEarnedBy()` reads only what is stored about the game, so that `app:trophies:award` gives it for the games played before (what is not stored yet cannot be given for them). Humans only and no replay are checked by `GameTrophies`; being played to its end (`VictoryCondition::isPlayedToTheEnd()`) is the choice of the rule.
  4. Add its value to `TrophyId` (`front/src/api/client.ts`: a test fails otherwise) and its name and goal to `front/src/data/trophies.ts`.
  5. Test it in `tests/Service/Trophy/GameTrophiesTest.php` (earned, and not earned), and update the count of `TrophyCaseTest`; `TrophyRulesTest` checks that each case has its class.
  6. Once deployed, run `app:trophies:award` in production (`--dry-run` first).

  A trophy earned elsewhere than at the end of a game (an account created, a deck imported, a game watched) has no class of `src/Trophy`: the service that sees it happen calls `TrophyCase::award()` (without a game), its own backfill is to write, and the end of a game does not announce it (`TrophyAnnouncement` shows those of the game only).
- Card database (`Service/CardDatabase/CardDatabaseImporter`, `app:cards:import`): the cycles, the packs (each naming its cycle, `cycle_id`), the cards as printed, the printings, the external cards and the starter decks, read from the JSON files of `fixtures/`; what exists is updated, what is missing is created, nothing is deleted, so that it runs again on a database in use (each start in production). The fixtures load the cards the same way (`AppFixtures`), then the users of development (`UserFixtures`).
- Matchmaking: a user who plays a game that has not ended (the replays left out) finishes it before joining the queue or facing the house bot, so that nobody plays two games at once; a player whose opponent has left ends the game themselves (see `game-flow.md`, "Abandoned games"). `Service/MatchMaking/FifoMatchMakingService` pairs the two candidates of an invitation together only, whoever came first (a friendly game), the two players of a match of a tournament together only (a standard game, see "Tournaments"), and the others in their order of arrival, holding the lock of the queue (`CandidateRepository::withQueueLock()`, a PostgreSQL advisory lock); `Service/GameLauncher/AutomaticGameLauncher` creates the game through the bus (`CreateGame`, then `JoinGame` for each candidate: recorded as events, the game starts once both have joined) and removes the candidates. It runs when a user joins the queue (`MatchMakingQueue`, for the HTTP API and the MCP tools), or with `app:matchmaking`.
- Tournaments (`Service/Tournament`; the decisions and the steps to come: `HANDOFF.md`, "Tournaments"): any account (not a guest, not a bot: `User::canTakePartInTournaments()`) creates a tournament with its start (`TournamentOrganizer::create()`), checked against the clock: on the hour or the half hour (in UTC, `Tournament::START_STEP_MINUTES`), at least 12 hours ahead (`MINIMUM_NOTICE`), its organizer organizing no other tournament still to be played, and no other tournament (not cancelled) beginning within 12 hours of it on the whole site (`MINIMUM_SPACING`), so that two tournaments never overlap; the rules of the calendar are checked under an advisory lock of their own (`TournamentRepository::withCalendarLock()`, another key than the queue's). Creating it does not register its organizer, who cancels it (`cancel()`) until it locks, one hour before it begins (`LOCK_LEAD`). An account registers with one of the decks it can use (`TournamentRegistrar::register()`, checked no more than for a standard game: `DeckRepository::findUsable()`), changes its deck by registering again, and unregisters, until it locks (unregistering stays allowed once it is cancelled), 64 players at most (`MAX_ENTRANTS`); the row of the tournament is locked meanwhile (`findForUpdate()`), so that two players registering at once do not pass the limit. A deck registered in a tournament still to be played is not deleted (`DeckDeleter`: `DeckInTournamentException`), one registered in a tournament that has ended or was cancelled is only marked deleted, like a deck played. Deleting an account unregisters it from the tournaments it can still leave (open and not locked, or cancelled: `TournamentEntryRepository::deleteLeavableOfUser()`); the tournaments it organizes go on, their organizer anonymized. A tournament is added to a calendar with its iCalendar file (`TournamentCalendar`, `eluceo/ical`): one event at its start, in UTC, ending 3 hours later (`LENGTH`: a game has no time limit, the end is only there for the calendars), with a reminder when it locks (one hour before), its organizer and the link to its page; its uid is the id of the tournament (`<id>@kyuden.app`), so that a calendar importing the file again updates the same event, and a cancelled tournament keeps its file, marked `STATUS:CANCELLED`. Running them (`TournamentRunner`, through the format of the tournament: `Format/TournamentFormatInterface`, found by `Format/TournamentFormats`; single elimination, `SingleEliminationFormat`): the task `Task/RunTournaments` of the worker, every minute (`App\Schedule`), moves on the tournaments whose time has come (`TournamentRepository::findIdsToRun()`), each in its own transaction, its row locked, under the lock of the queue. At H-1 a tournament locks: fewer than 2 players cancels it, else the size of its bracket is the smallest power of 2 holding them and its first round is drawn with its generator (`Tournament::createRandomizer()`, from its seed: the same draw each time), the byes spread over as many matches (fewer byes than players: a bye never meets a bye), kept secret while its current round is 0. At H its first round begins: its pairings revealed, its byes won at once, as a match against a deleted account (forfeit). The players of a match join the queue with it (`MatchMakingQueue::joinMatch()`, with the deck of their registration, until 10 minutes after the start of the round, replacing any other wait), and `FifoMatchMakingService` pairs them together only; `AutomaticGameLauncher` makes the game a standard game (ranked, with a mulligan) and the game of the match. Once the 10 minutes have passed, the task ends the matches without a game: the player waiting in the queue wins by forfeit, neither leaves no result (both eliminated). The end of a game (`GameWonEvent`, `GameEndedWithoutWinnerEvent`) records the result of its match (`EventListener/TournamentGameEndedListener`, in the transaction of the message that ends it: a replay has its own id and no match), a game without a winner eliminating both players. Once every match of a round has a result, the next round begins (single elimination: the table t takes the winners of the tables 2t-1 and 2t, a winner alone has a bye, no winner leaves no match), or the tournament ends with the winner of its final (none when no player is left). A deleted account forfeits (`withdraw()`, after `AccountDeleter` has conceded its games): the match of its current round without a game, and the matches of the rounds to come once they begin.
- Spectators (`Service/Spectating/SpectatorRegistry`): an account reading a game it does not play (its view, its log or its spectators) watches it, until it leaves or has not read it for 45 seconds (`TIMEOUT`: the web client reads a game at least every 15 seconds, the long-poll of the log lasts 30 at most); a game that has ended is not watched. `SpectatorRepository` writes in SQL (an upsert: two reads at once); whoever reads the game removes the spectators who stopped reading, and a spectator reading a game removes those of every game not read for an hour (`PURGE_AFTER`: ended, abandoned). Each arrival and departure is announced to the players and the spectators (`SpectatorsUpdatedNotification`, `spectators_updated` on `/games/{id}`, with the id of the game only), who read the spectators again; `GameUpdatedNotification` also goes to the spectators. A deleted account no longer watches anything.
- Worker: the tasks done out of the requests are messages of `src/Task` (`Task/TaskInterface`, each with its handler beside it, registered on `task.bus` only: `#[AsMessageHandler(bus: 'task.bus')]`), dispatched on `task.bus` (no middleware of the actions but `doctrine_ping_connection`: a task dispatches the actions it needs on `default.bus`, each in its own transaction) and routed to the transport `async` (Doctrine, the table `messenger_messages`, the worker woken at once by `LISTEN`/`NOTIFY`), which the worker consumes (the service `worker` in development and in production, from the image of the application). The recurring tasks are on the schedule of the application (`App\Schedule`, Symfony Scheduler, stateful in `cache.app`: running the tournaments, ending the abandoned games, deleting the replays nobody reads and the expired sessions), whose transport `scheduler_default` the same worker consumes: `messenger:consume async scheduler_default --bus=task.bus` (a message of the schedule carries no bus: every message received goes to `task.bus`). A task holds ids and reads the state again, and may be handled twice (a worker killed while handling it gets it again after 5 minutes, `redeliver_timeout`; a failure is retried 3 times, then kept in the transport `failed`): its handler does nothing when there is nothing left to do. The worker stops after an hour (and after 256 MB in production) and is started again by Docker; it keeps the code it loaded (`make worker-restart` in development). In the tests, `async` is in memory: a test reads the tasks sent from the transport and invokes their handler.
- Announcements on Discord (`Service/Discord/GameAnnouncer`): every game that starts and can be watched (against the house bot and friendly ones included, not the replays) is posted in a channel of the official Discord server, through its webhook (`DISCORD_GAMES_WEBHOOK_URL`, a secret of `symfony.env`; empty, the default: no announcement): an embed with the names at court of the players (their markdown escaped, mentioning nobody: `allowed_mentions`), the clans of their strongholds, the bots and the friendly games, linking to the game on the site (`SITE_URL`, `https://kyuden.app` in production). Once the last player has joined, `GameUpdatedNotificationMiddleware` sends the task `Task/AnnounceGame` to the worker, after the commit, so that Discord never slows a request and a failure of it is retried; the worker posts nothing for a game that has ended meanwhile.
- Notifications: `NotificationInterface` objects sent through `NotifierInterface`; `MercureNotifier` publishes them as private updates of the hub, `LoggerNotifier` logs them.
- Security: stateless access-token authentication: `Security/AccessTokenHandler` finds the `AccessToken` by the hash of the token, with its `User` (a single query; an unknown token gets a 401, the key of an invitation no longer valid and an expired session too), records the use of a session (`lastUsedAt`, at most once an hour, in a statement of its own: each use extends it) and keeps it for the request (`Security/CurrentAccessToken`). The task `Task/DeleteExpiredSessions` of the worker, every hour (`App\Schedule`), deletes the expired sessions (`Security/ExpiredSessionsDeleter`). A user is matched to the player of a game through `Player::user`. `Security/AccessTokenIssuer` issues the tokens (random, prefixed `l5r_`, returned once). The password of a human only gets a session token (`POST /api/sessions`); the API also issues one to a guest (`POST /api/guests`) and to an account created (`POST /api/accounts`). The bot of an account and the key of an invitation come with the invitation of the bot; other bots and their API keys are created by console commands (`app:user:create`, with `--username` for a human who signs in: the password is asked; `app:token:create`, `app:token:revoke`), and the fixtures hold users with known tokens and passwords (`DataFixtures/UserFixtures`, in clear, development and tests only, their tokens never expiring: last used in 2100; john and jane have an account, with a preferred clan and their copy of its starter deck, and the decks of `fixtures/user_decks` (one file per deck, `{"owner", "name", "cards"}`, not read by `app:cards:import`, legal decks with as many cards needing special actions as possible: Crab/Phoenix with Seeker of Earth and Scorpion/Lion with Keeper of Air for john, Dragon/Scorpion with Keeper of Void and Unicorn/Crane with Seeker of Void for jane: the four of them need every special message a script names), harry and sally are guests).
- Mercure: every topic is private (`Notification/Topics`): `/games/{id}` (`game_updated`: its id and version, no data of the game; `spectators_updated`: its id) and `/users/{id}` (`game_started`). A subscriber sends a token (`Notifier/SubscriptionFactory`, `GET /api/self/subscription`) whose `subscribe` grant is the topic of the user (`exact`) and a URL pattern of the topics of their games (`urlpattern`, `/users/{id}/games/:gameId`); an update of a game also carries that topic for each of its players and spectators (`Topics::ofGame()`), so that the token of a user reads every game they play or watch, joined before or after it was issued, and no other. The hub speaks the protocol 1.0 (no compatibility mode), and so does Symfony (`config/packages/mercure.yaml`): the tokens are RFC 9068 access tokens (`at+jwt`, `authorization_details`), signed with `MERCURE_JWT_SECRET`, from the issuer `https://kyuden.app` that the hub trusts (its `issuer` block) for the resource identifier the hub is given (`resource_identifier`, also the base of the relative topics and patterns), the same wherever it is reached; the subject of a subscriber token is its user. The protocol accepts no token in the URL, and browsers cannot set headers on an `EventSource`: `GET /api/self/subscription` sets the token in a cookie (`mercure_access_token`, the `cookie_name` of the hub and of the bundle: not prefixed with `__Secure-`, so that plain HTTP keeps it, secure over HTTPS), HTTP only, `SameSite=Strict`, on the path of the hub, for an hour; the browser sends it to the hub with the subscription (`match` for each topic, `last_event_id` to resume). The hub is the one FrankenPHP embeds, on the origin of the site: the server publishes to it inside its container (`MERCURE_URL`), the browsers subscribe to `MERCURE_PUBLIC_URL`, a relative URL (no anonymous subscriber, no CORS).
